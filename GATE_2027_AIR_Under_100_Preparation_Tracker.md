## 4. Simple Topic Status Codes

Use these codes in your diary instead of decorating every page.

- `NS` — Not started
- `T` — Theory completed
- `Q` — Basic questions completed
- `P` — PYQs attempted and analyzed
- `R1` — First revision completed
- `R2` — Second revision completed
- `TST` — Topic/subject test completed
- `W` — Weak topic; needs more practice
- `M` — Mistake logged
- `→` — Carry forward to another date

A topic is **not mastered just because the lecture is finished**. Mark it strong when you can recall the concept, solve representative questions independently, handle PYQs, and retain it during later revision.

## 8. Revision Schedule

Use spaced revision and adjust it based on how well you remember a topic.

- **R0:** Same day — recall key ideas and solve a few questions.
- **R1:** About 1 day later.
- **R2:** About 7 days later.
- **R3:** About 21–30 days later.
- **R4:** During mixed revision and mock-test preparation.

### Topic revision record

- [ ] R0 — Same day
- [ ] R1 — Next day
- [ ] R2 — Around 7 days later
- [ ] R3 — Around 21–30 days later

## 10. Test and Mock Analysis

After each test, classify questions:

- **A — Correct and confident:** Maintain through revision.
- **B — Correct but guessed/uncertain:** Relearn or verify the concept.
- **C — Wrong despite knowing the concept:** Check calculation, reading, and execution.
- **D — Wrong because the concept was unknown:** Schedule concept study and practice.

After the test:
- [ ] Review every wrong answer
- [ ] Review guessed or low-confidence correct answers
- [ ] Record avoidable errors
- [ ] Identify time-consuming question types
- [ ] Reattempt selected questions without the solution
- [ ] Schedule weak topics for revision
- [ ] Record score and accuracy for comparison

Do not judge progress from one mock alone. Look for a trend across several tests under comparable conditions.

## 11. Weekly Study Structure

### Monday–Saturday
For the main topic:
1. Theory and concept recall
2. Basic practice questions
3. Topic-wise PYQs
4. Mistake analysis
5. Short revision of older topics

Include a manageable amount of aptitude/math and DSA practice according to your overall plan. Avoid making every subject a major task every day.

### Sunday
- Weekly revision
- Mixed PYQs
- Topic/subject test
- Test analysis
- Mistake notebook
- Next week's plan

Keep buffer time for unfinished work. If a task is repeatedly carried forward, reduce its size or reconsider the plan.

## 12. What to Do for a Strong AIR Under 100 Attempt

- [ ] Learn concepts actively; pause lectures and recall what you learned.
- [ ] Start PYQs while studying each topic rather than postponing them until the whole syllabus is over.
- [ ] Practice questions independently before looking at solutions.
- [ ] Keep concise notes, formula sheets, and a mistake notebook.
- [ ] Reattempt wrong and uncertain questions.
- [ ] Revise topics at spaced intervals.
- [ ] Take topic tests, subject tests, and full-length mocks as preparation progresses.
- [ ] Analyze each test and act on the analysis.
- [ ] Track accuracy, coverage, revision, and test trends—not only study hours.
- [ ] Protect adequate sleep, breaks, and sustainable focus.
- [ ] Use the official syllabus and exam information to confirm coverage and dates.

## 13. What NOT to Do

- [ ] Do not keep changing teachers or resources without a clear reason.
- [ ] Do not create a completely new timetable every few days.
- [ ] Do not spend all study time watching lectures.
- [ ] Do not copy a solution immediately; try, use a hint, then reconstruct the solution yourself.
- [ ] Do not count a topic as complete just because notes or lectures are finished.
- [ ] Do not skip revision to chase 100% first-pass syllabus completion.
- [ ] Do not compare your progress with other students' unverified claims.
- [ ] Do not sacrifice sleep consistently to increase study hours.
- [ ] Do not take mocks without analyzing them.
- [ ] Do not hide incomplete work; reschedule it honestly.

---

## 14. Final Daily and Weekly Questions

Every night, ask:

1. Did I complete today's most important work?
2. What can I now solve independently that I couldn't solve before?
3. What mistake did I understand today?
4. What is the first task for tomorrow?

Every Sunday, ask:

1. Is my accuracy improving?
2. Are my PYQ and revision coverage increasing?
3. Are repeated mistakes decreasing?
4. What do my test results say about my weak areas?
5. What one change will improve next week?

## The System

**PLAN → EXECUTE → MEASURE → ANALYZE → CORRECT → REPEAT**

An AIR under 100 is an ambitious target, not a guaranteed outcome. Use this diary to make preparation measurable and to keep improving the quality of your work.
